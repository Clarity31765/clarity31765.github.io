Why Au and Ta ICP-MS standards go bad in about a week

Gold sticks to the bottle, tantalum needs fluoride, and the two together push up the background at m/z 197. What the literature says, and what to change.

By Oliver Lee, October 2026

The problem

In a lab running ICP-MS on high-purity process chemicals, the low-level gold (Au) and tantalum (Ta) working standards stopped giving reliable results about a week after preparation. At the same time, the Au background went up.

Working conditions:

  • Containers: PFA
  • Matrix: 2% HNO₃, with no HCl or HF

The question was simple: is this expected, and if so, what is the fix?

Short answer

Yes, it is expected. Under these conditions both elements are outside their stable range, and the two problems are linked.

Cause Fix
Au Sticks to container walls in nitric acid Use an HCl matrix (about 1% v/v)
Ta Not held in solution without fluoride Raise HNO₃ to 5%; if needed, add trace HF (≤0.1% v/v)
Au background Au released from walls and tubing, plus TaO⁺ at mass 197 Sulfur-based rinse; O₂ reaction gas for TaO⁺

1. Gold: lost to the container wall

The standards supplier Inorganic Ventures reports that, at ppb levels in 1% HNO₃ in polyethylene, only two elements are incompatible: Au and Hg. Both chemically adsorb onto the container wall and are not stable for 24 hours.

The loss depends on concentration, because the wall can take up only a limited amount:

  • 100 ppb Au can be used the next day, but not after 72 hours.
  • 1000 ppm Au in nitric acid stays stable for more than 12 months.
  • The supplier estimates that a PE container surface can take up about 100 µg of Au before adsorption stops. They note this is deduced, not validated.

Fix: prepare Au in an HCl matrix. The supplier expects 1% v/v HCl to remove the problem. They use 10% themselves but consider that more than needed.

2. Tantalum: needs fluoride to stay in solution

Ta, like Nb, is kept in solution as a fluoride complex. Commercial Ta stock standards are supplied with HF for this reason.

Inorganic Ventures has seen low-ppb Ta become unstable within weeks when diluted only in 2% HNO₃, which is exactly the condition here. Their advice, in order:

  1. Raise HNO₃ to 5%.
  2. If that is not enough, add trace HF (0.1% v/v or less).
  3. Clean preparation containers hard: leach in 5% HNO₃ at 60 °C for at least 60 hours, then rinse well. Mixed standards below 100 ppb often benefit from this.

3. Why the Au background goes up

Two mechanisms, which can act at the same time:

Memory effect. Au that adsorbed onto the walls and the sample path is slowly released into later solutions, including blanks. This shows up as a rising baseline at m/z 197.

TaO⁺ interference. Au has only one isotope, ¹⁹⁷Au. The oxide ion ¹⁸¹Ta¹⁶O⁺ has the same nominal mass. A system that contains Ta, from a mixed standard or from Ta memory, will add signal at 197.

Fixes from the literature:

  • Rinse: Chen et al. (JAAS, 2000) showed that sulfur complexing agents clear Au memory quickly: 2% L-cysteine cleared 1 µg/mL Au in under a minute, and thiourea cleared 100 µg/mL in about two minutes.
  • Interference: a 2015 RSC Advances paper used O₂ reaction gas to move TaO⁺ to higher oxides such as TaO₂⁺. Au does not react with O₂, so it stays at 197.

4. Applied to our conditions

Our condition What the literature says Change
Au in 2% HNO₃ Low ppb: not stable for 24 h; 100 ppb: not stable at 72 h Prepare Au separately in ~1% HCl
Ta in 2% HNO₃, no HF Unstable within weeks at low ppb 5% HNO₃ first, then trace HF if needed
Au and Ta in the same standard Ta memory can add TaO⁺ at m/z 197 Separate Au and Ta standards; O₂ mode for Au
Nitric acid rinse only Does not remove adsorbed Au well Add an L-cysteine or thiourea rinse step
PFA containers No published PFA data found Run our own short stability check (see below)

5. What I could not confirm

All the stability numbers above were measured in polyethylene (PE / LDPE) containers. I did not find any published data that measures Au or Ta adsorption on PFA.

PFA is generally cleaner and less adsorptive than PE, so the problem may be smaller in PFA. That is a reasonable expectation, but it is not a measured result. The right next step is a short in-house check: prepare Au and Ta at the working level in each candidate matrix, and measure on day 0, 1, 3 and 7.
