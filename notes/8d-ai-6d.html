An 8D written by AI alone is a 6D

8D starts by forming a team and ends by recognizing it. An AI writing alone drops the first and last disciplines. What published studies say AI can and cannot take over.

By Oliver Lee, November 2026

AI tools that fill in D0 through D8 from a complaint description are everywhere now, and the reports they produce look convincing. But look again at how 8D is built, and something is missing.

An 8D written by AI alone is a 6D: D1 and D8 are gone.

D1 forms the team. D8 recognizes what the team did. With no team, there is nothing real to put in either.

8D is a team method

Ford developed 8D and published it in 1987 as the Team Oriented Problem Solving (TOPS) manual; D0 was added in the mid-1990s.1 The team is in the name. In Ford's G8D, D1 is establishing a team with product and process knowledge, and D8 is congratulating the main contributors and recognizing the team's collective effort.2

StepWhatKey point
D0Prepare, emergency responseDecide whether an 8D is warranted
D1Form the teamCross-functional, with product and process knowledge
D2Describe the problem5W2H and is/is-not, in measurable terms
D3Interim containmentProtect the customer while the cause is unknown
D4Root causeVerify occurrence and escape causes, each with data
D5Choose permanent actionVerify effect and side effects before rollout
D6Implement, validateSame metric as D2, then lift containment
D7Prevent recurrenceUpdate FMEA, control plan, standards; read across
D8Recognize the team, closeCredit the team, customer sign-off

Some 8D training puts it as rule number one: "If there's no team, it's not an 8D."3 Others warn that skipping D8 teaches people that problem solving is punishment, and the next 8D team gets harder to staff.4

AI fills the boxes

One published AI 8D instruction set tells the model not to ask for missing data but to infer it and label it as an assumption, and insists that D8 must always be written in full.5 So with no team at all, D1 gets a plausible roster and D8 a plausible thank-you. It has the form of an 8D and the substance of a 6D.

The bigger risk is D4. A classic 8D failure is writing a plausible root cause just to fill the box,6 and one quality engineer writing an 8D alone tends to produce a root cause that is incomplete, convenient or wrong.7 Plausible sentences are what AI does best. 8D manages an investigation; it does not tell you how to find the cause,8 so a perfect-looking report can still carry the wrong one.

Where the research is

I read the two studies that combine AI and 8D directly. They agree: no discipline has been shown to be replaceable by AI. Everything that worked was assistance with a human approving each step.

  • Adadi et al. (2026) reviewed 2023–2025 literature: of 47 included papers, 6 dealt with AI in 8D directly. Their automotive case used AI vision inspection. D4–D6 were validated, D3 partly, and D1, D2, D7 and D8 remain conceptual, drawn from the literature. The authors state that AI recommends and humans keep final authority.9
  • Ravi and Gopalakrishnan (2026) built per-discipline LLM agents on a knowledge graph of 412 past 8D cases. Every discipline had a mandatory approval gate with a written rationale, and every AI hypothesis had to link to a source case. With 40 engineers, root-cause accuracy on an automotive paint delamination case was 84% versus 61%, though some baselines came from the literature rather than a concurrent control group. Team formation and accountability stayed with people.10

Both studies come from automotive and card manufacturing. I found no validated results from semiconductor fabs or materials.

D4 from a materials quality seat

In 2019, one batch of a supplier's photoresist at TSMC Fab 14B contained an abnormally treated component that formed a foreign polymer, and it showed up only when 12/16 nm yield dropped. TSMC put the first-quarter revenue impact at about US$550 million and said it would tighten inline inspection and incoming material control.11 On the public facts, the defect passed three detection layers: supplier outgoing, fab incoming and inline.

In materials analysis, the escape causes I check first are sample representativeness and measurement method. A CoA sample drawn from the tank before filling cannot show contamination picked up in the filling line. Heavily diluting a CMP slurry sample hides exactly the large particles that scratch.12 That kind of judgment doesn't come from searching past cases. It comes from someone in the room who knows where the sample came from. That is why D1 matters.

How I would use AI

AI does

  • D2 problem statement draft
  • D4 past-case search, hypothesis list
  • D6 monitoring
  • D7 read-across search
  • Report writing, translation

AI checks

  • Missing escape cause
  • Weak causes like "operator error"
  • "Retrain" as the only action
  • Effectiveness claimed without data

People do

  • D1 team formation
  • D4 root cause confirmation
  • Gate approvals with rationale
  • D8 recognition, customer closure

The design rules can come straight from the second study: approval gates per discipline, a written rationale at each gate, and every AI hypothesis tied to evidence. Its results also rested on 412 structured past cases. Before adding AI, check whether your own past 8Ds are usable data.

Limits of this note

  • The research is thin. Few studies address AI and 8D directly, and none come from semiconductors.
  • No company data. All cases and figures are from public sources.
